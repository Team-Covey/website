From airliner to simulator

The story we are preserving

The Jumbo Project is bringing a genuine Boeing 747-400 flight deck back to life in Australia. Its supplied history identifies the cockpit as JA8961—ANA’s final passenger 747—and the team is checking the surviving paperwork as part of the restoration.

i

Provenance in progress. JA8961’s airline history is well documented. The simulator was supplied with this identity, and its original registration records are among the material now being catalogued by the project team.

  1. 011993–2014

    JA8961 in airline service

    JA8961 was an ANA Boeing 747-481D built for Japan’s high-frequency domestic network. Registered in May 1993, the aircraft’s distinctive domestic configuration omitted winglets and was strengthened for repeated short-haul cycles.

    On 31 March 2014, JA8961 operated ANA’s final scheduled 747 passenger service: NH126 from Naha to Tokyo Haneda. It closed more than three decades of ANA Jumbo operations with the aircraft that had carried generations of travellers across Japan.

  2. 02The simulator story

    A second life in Texas

    At the end of the donor aircraft’s flying life, Universal Asset Management (UAM) dismantled the 747 at its aircraft recycling facility in Tupelo, Mississippi. The cockpit section was separated from the airframe and preserved rather than being lost with the rest of the aircraft.

    The flight deck was then restored as a highly detailed simulator built around original Boeing 747-400 equipment. It later lived in an aircraft hangar and then a warehouse in Austin, Texas, with its switches, lights and controls interfaced to Aerowinx PSX.

    Team Covey acquired the simulator in May 2026. The flight deck was carefully decommissioned and dismantled over seven days, then loaded into a 40-foot high-cube container for the journey to Australia. Reassembly is the next chapter.
